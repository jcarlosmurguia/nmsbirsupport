Dept. of Energy · Genesis Mission

Thirteen days left on DOE’s $10M Genesis Mission Phase I pitch

DOE’s FY26 Genesis Mission Phase I competition anticipates roughly 40 Phase I awards from a $10 million pool. All four topic areas apply artificial intelligence to accelerate science: biotechnology, quantum computing and networking, AI-driven materials design, and autonomous AI-powered laboratories — a near-exact overlay on New Mexico’s national-lab strengths. The program is pitch-first: project pitches are due September 10, 2026 at 2:00pm ET, and only approved pitches are invited to file a full Phase I application. Pitches are scored on mission alignment, technical promise, commercialization potential and team qualifications, with hard word limits, so this is a short document that rewards precision over volume. Eligibility is the standard SBA test — for-profit, 500 or fewer employees, majority U.S.-owned, work performed domestically.

National Institutes of Health

NIH’s Phase IIB Strategic Breakthrough Award shares the same September receipt date

PA-27-101, released May 28, 2026, is NIH’s implementation of the reauthorization’s new “strategic breakthrough” vehicle: a second Phase II award for projects with strong commercial potential that need more time and money to finish, with total support capped at $30,000,000. Two conditions narrow the field sharply — applicants must have previously held an NIH SBIR or STTR Phase II award, and must secure 100% third-party matching funds. It runs on the same standard dates as the parent omnibus: September 5, 2026 (accepted through September 8), then January 5 and April 5, 2027. A September submission means merit review in November, council in January 2027 and an earliest start of April 2027 — plan cash flow accordingly.

Congress · SBA

The five-year reauthorization is the context for every deadline on this page

S. 3971, the Small Business Innovation and Economic Security Act, was signed April 13, 2026, extending SBIR and STTR through September 30, 2031 — the longest extension in the programs’ history, and the end of a roughly six-month lapse that began when the prior authorization expired September 30, 2025. The Senate passed it by voice vote March 3 and the House 345–41 on March 17. The law authorizes roughly $6 billion and adds the Phase II “Strategic Breakthrough” vehicle with a $30 million ceiling. Practically, it is why NIH, NSF, DOE and the defense agencies all have live solicitations this month, and why multi-year R&D planning is defensible again.

SBA · participating agencies

Foreign-risk due diligence now touches every application — and proposal caps arrive in FY2027

Under the reauthorization, every SBIR and STTR application goes through a due-diligence review covering cybersecurity practices, patent portfolios, employee backgrounds and financial ties to foreign countries of concern, with denials keyed to UFLPA, Chinese Military-Industrial Complex, Section 889 and Commerce Entity List designations. An agency denying an application on security-risk grounds must give written notice of the basis, and a denial does not permanently bar future eligibility. Two other provisions bite next fiscal year: agencies with SBIR budgets above $100 million must carve out 0.5% for Strategic Breakthrough awards, and beginning in FY2027 each agency sets its own per-solicitation or per-topic proposal caps, replacing the old lifetime-cap approach and rewarding selectivity over volume. Companies with foreign investors, foreign co-founders or offshore development work should get their disclosure story straight before submitting.
