NASA

NASA is on a rolling BAA — no fixed annual deadline to plan around

On April 17, 2026 NASA released a Broad Agency Announcement valid through September 30, 2027, replacing the single-window solicitation the space-tech community organized its year around for two decades. Proposals now arrive through phased appendix releases instead of one annual deadline. The PY2026 Phase I appendices — 2026A SBIR, 2026B SBIR and 2026B STTR — closed May 21, 2026, so today's action is monitoring the PY2026 hub for the next appendix and completing ProSAMS registration in advance. Under a rolling BAA the announcement-to-deadline window is short, and registration delay is what disqualifies firms, not proposal quality. Maximum award values rose 50% under the new BAA: Phase I now tops out at $225,000 and Phase II at $1,275,000 — a material change to the competitive math for New Mexico space and sensing companies.

Congress · SBA

Reauthorization in force through FY2031 — and the FY2026 clock has six weeks left

The Small Business Innovation and Economic Security Act (S. 3971) passed the Senate March 3 and the House March 17, and was signed into law April 13, 2026, extending SBIR and STTR through September 30, 2031 and ending the six-month lapse that began September 30, 2025. It is the longest extension in the programs' history and authorizes roughly $6B. With FY2026 ending September 30, agencies are compressing solicitations to obligate funds — which is exactly why the August and September deadlines on this page are stacked so tightly. The Act also lets agencies carry unspent FY2026 SBIR/STTR funds into FY2027, which should soften the usual end-of-year cliff.

Three provisions matter most for applicants. First, a new “Strategic Breakthrough” Phase II vehicle authorizing awards up to $30M, requiring private or non-SBIR matching funds; only agencies spending over $100M annually on extramural SBIR may offer it, which realistically means DoD, NIH, NASA, DOE and possibly NSF. Second, expanded foreign-risk and supply-chain screening, including affiliations in countries of concern. Third, and most consequential for planning: beginning in FY2027, every participating agency must cap how many Phase I and Phase II proposals a single firm may submit — per company, per solicitation or per topic. Firms that have relied on submitting broadly and playing the odds need a different strategy starting next fiscal year. From FY2027, every proposal has to count.
