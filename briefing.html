Dept. of Energy · Office of Science

DOE SBIR/STTR pipeline broad and open alongside FY26 restart

Beyond Genesis Mission, DOE runs one of the deepest SBIR/STTR pipelines in government — roughly 400 Phase I and 200 Phase II awards a year on a $300M+ budget, with 60+ technical topics and 250+ subtopics. Topics span the Office of Science and applied-energy offices (EERE, Fossil Energy, Nuclear Energy, ARPA-E): clean energy, advanced nuclear, grid modernization, energy storage, carbon capture, hydrogen, fusion, high-energy-physics instrumentation, advanced computing, and critical-minerals processing. DOE publishes topics about a month before each Funding Opportunity Announcement, then gives roughly three weeks for a two-page letter of intent — a deep, recurring well for New Mexico's energy and computing firms.

NIH · Seed

NIH SBIR/STTR omnibus reopened — first receipt date September 8

After a freeze that expired every open NOFO last November and left no active omnibus this spring, NIH has reissued its parent SBIR/STTR series following April's reauthorization. The first standard receipt date is September 5, 2026, which shifts to Tuesday, September 8 because the 5th falls on a Saturday before Labor Day; later windows are January 5 and April 5, 2027. Congress · SBA

SBIR/STTR reauthorized through September 30, 2031

The Small Business Innovation and Economic Security Act (S. 3971) was signed into law April 13, 2026, extending both programs through FY2031 and ending the roughly six-month lapse that began September 30, 2025. It authorizes about $6 billion in program funding and lets agencies carry unobligated FY2026 funds into FY2027. With the fiscal year ending September 30, agencies are racing to publish and close solicitations, compressing this year's deadlines closer together than usual.

Two changes matter for applicants: a new “Strategic Breakthrough” allocation letting large-budget agencies fund milestone-based awards up to $30M per company, and expanded national-security & supply-chain due diligence — including screening for foreign affiliations in countries of concern. Beginning in FY2027, agencies will also set their own caps on how many Phase I and Phase II proposals a firm may submit.
