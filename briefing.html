National Science Foundation · America’s Seed Fund

NSF’s November 4 window is close enough that the Project Pitch clock starts this month

NSF restarted SBIR/STTR under solicitation NSF 26-510 with roughly $250 million spread across Phase I, Phase II, Fast-Track, supplements and Strategic Breakthrough awards. The first post-relaunch full-proposal deadline passed July 27; the next is November 4, 2026, followed by March 4, 2027 and then the first Wednesday in November and first Thursday in March annually. Phase I runs up to $305,000 and Phase II up to $1,250,000 over two years, with no matching-funds requirement — the most founder-friendly terms in the federal portfolio, and open to any deep-tech field, which is why it reaches all four NM FAST sectors. Project Pitch submissions reopened June 2 and an invited pitch is mandatory before a Phase I proposal, so a firm that has not yet pitched can still make November but should not let September pass first.

NASA’s rolling BAA: no annual deadline, so registration is the whole game

NASA released a Broad Agency Announcement on April 17, 2026 valid through September 30, 2027, replacing the single annual solicitation window the space-tech community planned around for years. Proposals now arrive through phased appendix releases. The PY2026 Phase I appendices — 2026A SBIR, 2026B SBIR and 2026B STTR — opened April 21 and closed May 21, 2026 at 5:00pm ET, so today's work is monitoring the PY2026 information hub for the next appendix and completing registration in advance. Under a rolling BAA the announcement-to-deadline window is short, and registration delay disqualifies more firms than proposal quality does. Maximum award values rose 50% under the new BAA — Phase I now tops out at $225,000 and Phase II at $1,275,000 — a material change to the competitive math for New Mexico space and sensing companies.

Congress · SBA

Six weeks to the end of FY2026 — and the submission caps that arrive with FY2027

The Small Business Innovation and Economic Security Act of 2026 (S. 3971) cleared the Senate March 3 and the House March 17, and was signed into law April 13, 2026, extending SBIR and STTR through September 30, 2031 and ending a six-month lapse in program authority that had halted new awards across federal agencies. The law authorizes roughly $6 billion. With FY2026 ending September 30, agencies are compressing solicitations to obligate funds, which is precisely why the deadlines on this page are stacked into the next five weeks — though the Act does let agencies carry unspent FY2026 SBIR/STTR funds into FY2027, which should soften the usual end-of-year cliff.

Three provisions matter most for planning. A new Phase II “Strategic Breakthrough” vehicle authorizes awards up to $30 million against matching private or non-SBIR funds. Mandatory foreign-risk screening now applies to every application, checking applicants against enumerated federal watchlists and examining foreign ownership, control, influence or affiliation — firms with foreign investors or offshore development should get ahead of the disclosure now rather than at award. And beginning in FY2027, each agency will set its own caps on how many Phase I and Phase II proposals a company may submit, per company, per solicitation or per topic. Firms that have relied on submission volume need a different strategy starting next October.
