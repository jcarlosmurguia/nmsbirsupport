DOE’s $10M Genesis Mission Phase I pitch closes September 10 — miss the pitch, lose the cycle

DOE opened its FY2026 Phase I Genesis Mission SBIR/STTR opportunity on July 22 under a new pitch-first process: a short pitch is due September 10, 2026 at 2:00pm ET, and only invited companies may submit a full Phase I application. Roughly 40 awards totaling about $10 million are planned across four AI-for-science topic areas — AI in biotechnology, quantum computing, materials design, and autonomous laboratories. AFWERX & SpaceWERX

AFWERX and SpaceWERX have replaced the propose-anything Open Topic with Focused Open Topics

Both offices reopened solicitations in April 2026 after the reauthorization restored program authority, but the model changed: the traditional Open Topic is gone for 2026 and has been replaced by Focused Open Topics, where the Air Force names the capability gap and leaves the solution approach open. AFWERX priorities are Autonomous Mass, Command & Control and Battle Management, Counter Incursion, and Agile Combat & Readiness; SpaceWERX adds on-orbit logistics, defensive cyber, cis-lunar space domain awareness, and novel sensing. Submissions run through DSIP and require a Government Engagement Memorandum confirming support from a valid Department of the Air Force customer — that memo is the long-pole item and cannot be produced in a weekend.

NSF’s next full-proposal window is November 4 — but the Project Pitch gate comes first

Under solicitation NSF 26-510, the next Phase I full-proposal window closes November 4, 2026, with a further window on March 4, 2027. SBA & federal agencies

Per-firm proposal caps arrive with FY2027 — the shotgun-submission strategy stops working next month

Beginning in fiscal year 2027, which starts October 1, agencies will set their own proposal caps limiting how many proposals a single business may submit on a per-company, per-solicitation or per-topic basis. Agencies were required to establish those limits no later than 90 days before the start of FY2027, so the numbers should be published or publishing now. Waivers exist for time-sensitive topics but may not cover more than 5% of an agency’s topics in a fiscal year. For New Mexico firms, the practical effect is that volume stops substituting for fit: fewer, better-targeted proposals is now the only available strategy.

Congress · Public Law (S. 3971)

Reauthorization background: programs run through September 30, 2031 after a six-month lapse

SBIR and STTR authority expired September 30, 2025 and stayed dark for roughly six months — the longest disruption in the programs’ 43-year history. The Small Business Innovation and Economic Security Act (S. 3971) passed the Senate by voice vote on March 3, 2026 and the House 345–41 on March 17, and was signed April 13, 2026, reauthorizing both programs through September 30, 2031. Alongside the extension it added national security reviews and foreign-risk due diligence requirements, directed SBA to push procurement center representatives toward maximum practicable Phase III transition, and created a Strategic Breakthrough Allocation letting agencies with SBIR budgets above $100 million devote up to 0.5% of extramural research budgets to awards of up to $30 million per project.
