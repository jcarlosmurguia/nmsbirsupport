Two weeks left on DOE’s $10M Genesis Mission Phase I pitch

DOE’s FY26 Genesis Mission Phase I competition anticipates roughly 40 Phase I awards totaling $10 million, with as much as $44 million in cumulative funding across the projects’ lifecycles. All four topic areas apply artificial intelligence to accelerate science: biotechnology, quantum computing and networking, AI-driven materials design, and autonomous AI-powered laboratories. The program is pitch-first — project pitches are due September 10, 2026 at 2:00pm ET through AMP, and only approved pitches are invited to file a full Phase I application. Pitches are scored on mission alignment, technical promise, commercialization potential and team qualifications, and the Pitch Development Guide asks four questions with hard word limits, so this is a short document that rewards precision over volume.

National Institutes of Health

NIH’s new Phase IIB Strategic Breakthrough Award shares the September 5 receipt date

PA-27-101, released May 28, 2026, is NIH’s implementation of the reauthorization’s new “strategic breakthrough” vehicle: a second Phase II award for projects with strong commercial potential that need more time and money to finish, with total support (direct, indirect and fee) capped at $30,000,000. Two conditions narrow the field sharply — applicants must have previously held an NIH SBIR or STTR Phase II award, and must secure 100% third-party matching funds for the proposed project. It runs on the same standard dates as the parent omnibus: September 5, 2026 (accepted through September 8), then January 5 and April 5, 2027. A September 5 submission means merit review in November, council in January 2027 and an earliest start of April 2027 — plan cash flow accordingly.

Congress · SBA

The five-year reauthorization is the context for every deadline on this page

S. 3971, the Small Business Innovation and Economic Security Act, was signed April 13, 2026 as Public Law 119-83, extending SBIR and STTR through September 30, 2031 — the longest extension in the programs’ history, and the end of a roughly six-month lapse that began when the prior authorization expired September 30, 2025. The Senate passed it by voice vote March 3 and the House 345–41 on March 17. The law authorizes roughly $6 billion, adds the Phase II “Strategic Breakthrough” vehicle with a $30 million ceiling, and expands foreign-risk screening. Practically, it is why NIH, NSF, DOE and the defense agencies all have live solicitations this month, and why multi-year R&D planning is defensible again.

SBA · participating agencies

Strategic Breakthrough set-asides and agency proposal caps both bite in FY2027

Three provisions of the reauthorization shape the next twelve months. Agencies with SBIR budgets above $100 million must set aside 0.5% for Strategic Breakthrough awards — NIH has already posted its version, and NSF, DOE, NASA and DoD implementations are expected to follow. Beginning in fiscal 2027 each agency also sets its own proposal caps, limiting how many Phase I and Phase II proposals a company may submit per solicitation or per topic, replacing the old lifetime-cap approach and rewarding selectivity over volume. And a carry-over provision lets agencies that end FY2026 with unspent SBIR/STTR funds use them in FY2027, preserving money idled during the lapse. Watch for individual agency cap announcements as FY2027 solicitations post.
