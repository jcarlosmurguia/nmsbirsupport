NASA released a Broad Agency Announcement on April 17, 2026 valid through September 30, 2027, replacing the single-window January solicitation the space-tech community planned around for two decades. Congress · SBA

Six weeks to the end of FY2026 — and the submission caps that arrive with FY2027

The Small Business Innovation and Economic Security Act (S. 3971) passed the Senate March 3 and the House 345–41 on March 17, and was signed into law April 13, 2026, extending SBIR and STTR through September 30, 2031 and ending the six-month lapse that began September 30, 2025. It is the longest extension in the programs' history and authorizes roughly $6B. With FY2026 ending September 30, agencies are compressing solicitations to obligate funds, which is precisely why the deadlines on this page are stacked into the next five weeks. The Act does let agencies carry unspent FY2026 SBIR/STTR funds into FY2027, which should soften the usual end-of-year cliff.

Three provisions matter most for planning. A new “Strategic Breakthrough” Phase II vehicle authorizes awards up to $30M against private or non-SBIR matching funds, available only from agencies spending over $100M annually on extramural SBIR — realistically DoD, NIH, NASA, DOE and possibly NSF. Foreign-risk and supply-chain screening expands, including scrutiny of affiliations in countries of concern, so firms with foreign investors or offshore development should get ahead of the disclosure now rather than at award. And beginning in FY2027, every participating agency must cap how many Phase I and Phase II proposals a single firm may submit — per company, per solicitation or per topic. Firms that have relied on volume need a different strategy starting next October.
