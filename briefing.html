NASA

NASA’s rolling BAA: no annual deadline, so registration is the whole game

NASA moved to a Broad Agency Announcement structure in 2026, replacing the single annual solicitation window the space-tech community planned around for years. Phase I subtopics for SBIR and STTR now arrive through phased appendix releases. The PY2026 Phase I appendices — 2026A SBIR, 2026B SBIR and 2026B STTR — closed May 21, 2026 at 5:00pm ET, so today's work is monitoring the PY2026 information hub for the next appendix and finishing ProSAMS registration in advance. Under a rolling BAA the announcement-to-deadline window is short, and registration delay disqualifies more firms than proposal quality does. Maximum award values rose 50% under the new structure — Phase I now tops out at $225,000 and Phase II at $1,275,000 — a material change to the competitive math for New Mexico space and sensing companies.

Congress · SBA

Thirty-nine days to the end of FY2026 — and the submission caps that arrive with FY2027

The Small Business Innovation and Economic Security Act of 2026 (S. 3971) cleared the Senate March 3 and the House March 17, and was signed into law April 13, 2026, extending SBIR and STTR through September 30, 2031 and ending a six-month lapse in program authority that had halted new awards across federal agencies. With FY2026 ending September 30, agencies are compressing solicitations to obligate funds — which is precisely why the deadlines on this page stack into the next five weeks — though the Act does let agencies carry unobligated FY2026 SBIR/STTR funds into FY2027, which should soften the usual end-of-year cliff.

Three provisions matter most for planning. A new Phase II “Strategic Breakthrough” funding vehicle carries a $30 million ceiling, with first solicitations expected late 2026 or early 2027. Increased foreign-risk screening now applies across applications, covering ownership, employee backgrounds and financial ties to countries of concern — firms with foreign investors or offshore development should get ahead of that disclosure now rather than at award. And beginning in fiscal year 2027, each agency will set its own proposal caps limiting how many Phase I and Phase II proposals a company may submit, on a per-company, per-solicitation or per-topic basis. Firms that have relied on submission volume need a different strategy starting next October.
