NASA

NASA’s rolling BAA: no annual deadline, so registration is the whole game

NASA released a Broad Agency Announcement on April 17, 2026 valid through September 30, 2027, replacing the single annual solicitation window the space-tech community planned around for years. Phase I subtopics for SBIR, STTR and SBIR Ignite now arrive through phased appendix releases, and proposal limits reset with each appendix. The PY2026 Phase I appendices — 2026A SBIR, 2026B SBIR and 2026B STTR — opened April 21 and closed May 21, 2026 at 5:00pm ET, so today's work is monitoring the PY2026 information hub for the next appendix and finishing ProSAMS registration in advance. Under a rolling BAA the announcement-to-deadline window is short, and registration delay disqualifies more firms than proposal quality does. Maximum award values rose 50% under the new BAA — Phase I now tops out at $225,000 and Phase II at $1,275,000 — a material change to the competitive math for New Mexico space and sensing companies.

Congress · SBA

Forty days to the end of FY2026 — and the submission caps that arrive with FY2027

The Small Business Innovation and Economic Security Act of 2026 (S. 3971) cleared the Senate March 3 and the House March 17 by 345 to 41, and was signed into law April 13, 2026, extending SBIR and STTR through September 30, 2031 and ending a six-month lapse in program authority that had halted new solicitations and awards across federal agencies. The law authorizes roughly $6 billion. With FY2026 ending September 30, agencies are compressing solicitations to obligate funds, which is precisely why the deadlines on this page stack into the next five weeks — though the Act does let agencies carry unobligated FY2026 funds into FY2027, which should soften the usual end-of-year cliff.

Three provisions matter most for planning. A new Phase II “Strategic Breakthrough” vehicle authorizes awards up to $30 million over 48 months against matching private or non-SBIR funds, with first solicitations expected late 2026 or early 2027. Mandatory foreign-risk screening now applies to every application, covering ownership, patents, employee backgrounds and financial ties to countries of concern — firms with foreign investors or offshore development should get ahead of that disclosure now rather than at award. And beginning in FY2027, each agency will set its own caps on how many Phase I and Phase II proposals a company may submit, per company, per solicitation or per topic. Firms that have relied on submission volume need a different strategy starting next October.
