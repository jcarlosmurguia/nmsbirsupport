NIH SBIR/STTR submissions reopen August 5 — first receipt date September 8

After a five-month freeze that expired every open NOFO last November and left no active omnibus this spring, NIH reopens its SBIR/STTR submission window on August 5, 2026 under the reset parent series (PA-27-100). The first standard receipt date is September 8, 2026 (shifted from Sep 5 for the weekend and Labor Day), followed by January 5 and April 5, 2027. Phase I awards run to roughly $323K and Phase II to about $2.15M, with a newly authorized Strategic Breakthrough lane up to $30M. Note HHS now caps submissions at 9 per small business per fiscal year across all activity codes — a strong but competitive fit for New Mexico's lab-adjacent life-science startups.

DARPA SBIR window closes Aug 19 — strategic topics live now

DARPA's July 22 SBIR/STTR release closes August 19, 2026 (about 15 days out) and includes sharply-scoped Defense Sciences topics such as FALCON (fusing efficient ML with large language models for interactive analysis of massive data) and “Art of Novel Signals” (temporal knowledge-graph forecasting from multilingual, multimodal data). Under the monthly-drip model DARPA has used since reauthorization, topics open and close on roughly four-week cycles, so New Mexico firms in signal processing, ML/autonomy, and rad-hard electronics have a fast-moving but recurring target. Confirm exact topic numbers and instructions on DSIP before drafting; DARPA does not extend SBIR deadlines under normal circumstances.

Congress · SBA

SBIR/STTR reauthorized through September 30, 2031

The Small Business Innovation and Economic Security Act (S. 3971) was signed into law April 13, 2026, extending both programs through FY2031 and ending the six-month lapse that began September 30, 2025. It authorizes roughly $6 billion in program funding and lets agencies carry unobligated FY2026 funds into FY2027.

Two changes matter for applicants: a new “Strategic Breakthrough” allocation letting large-budget agencies fund milestone-based awards up to $30M per company, and expanded national-security & supply-chain due diligence — including screening for foreign affiliations in countries of concern. Beginning in FY2027, agencies will also set their own caps on how many Phase I and Phase II proposals a firm may submit.
