National Institutes of Health

NIH’s reissued parent SBIR omnibus faces its first real deadline in 13 days

NIH reissued its parent SBIR omnibus as PA-27-100, reopening a roughly $1.4 billion small-business pipeline with Phase I/II caps raised to $3 million. The first standard receipt date is listed as September 5, but because that falls on a Saturday ahead of Labor Day, submissions are accepted through Tuesday, September 8, 2026. The new omnibus consolidates prior tracks and lets applicants propose — or decline to propose — clinical trials within a single NOFO, subject to individual institute rules; NIAMS, NIDCR, NCATS, ORIP and all FDA centers still do not accept clinical-trial applications, while NCI, NHLBI, NINDS, NIA and NIAID do. This is the first genuine test of the post-lapse NIH pipeline.

Dept. of Energy · Genesis Mission

DOE’s $10M Genesis Mission Phase I closes September 10

DOE opened a $10 million FY26 Phase I competition tied to the Genesis Mission, anticipating roughly 40 Phase I awards with as much as $44 million in cumulative funding across the projects’ lifecycles. The four topic areas all apply artificial intelligence to accelerate science: biotechnology, quantum computing and networking, AI-driven materials design, and autonomous AI-powered laboratories. Submissions are due no later than September 10, 2026 at 2:00pm ET, and the process front-loads a short competitive pitch — companies must first persuade DOE the idea belongs in the program before filing a full Phase I proposal. Eligibility is standard SBA: for-profit U.S. small business, 500 or fewer employees, work performed domestically.

Congress · SBA

The five-year reauthorization is the context for every deadline on this page

S. 3971, the Small Business Innovation and Economic Security Act, was signed April 13, 2026, extending SBIR and STTR through September 30, 2031 — the longest runway the programs have had in modern history, and the end of a 195-day lapse that began when the prior authorization expired September 30, 2025. The law authorizes roughly $6 billion, adds a new Phase II “Strategic Breakthrough” vehicle with a $30 million ceiling, and expands foreign-risk screening. Practically, it is why NIH, NSF, DOE and the defense agencies all have live solicitations again this month, and why multi-year R&D planning is defensible for the first time in several cycles.

SBA · participating agencies

Agency-set proposal caps arrive in FY2027 — and unspent FY2026 SBIR dollars carry over

Two provisions of the reauthorization matter for planning the next twelve months. First, beginning in fiscal year 2027 each agency sets its own proposal caps, limiting how many Phase I and Phase II proposals a company may submit on a per-company, per-solicitation or per-topic basis — replacing the old lifetime cap approach and rewarding selectivity over volume submission. Second, a carry-over provision lets agencies that finish FY2026 with unspent SBIR/STTR funds use them in FY2027, which should preserve money left idle during the lapse. Watch for individual agency cap announcements as FY2027 solicitations post.
