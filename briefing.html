NIH omnibus receipt date is eight days out — the most urgent bioscience deadline on this page

NIH reopened its small-business submission window on August 5, 2026 with a reissued parent series: PA-27-100 (SBIR), PA-27-102 (STTR), PA-27-101 (Phase IIB Strategic Breakthrough) and PAR-27-098 (Commercialization Readiness Pilot). DOE’s $10M Genesis Mission Phase I closes September 10 — AI for science across four topic areas

DOE announced the Genesis Mission SBIR/STTR Phase I opportunity on July 22, 2026, funding small businesses that apply artificial intelligence to accelerate discovery in biotechnology, quantum computing and networking, AI-driven materials design, and autonomous AI-powered laboratories. DOE expects roughly 40 awards from a $10 million pool, with the selected cohort potentially representing as much as $44 million in cumulative SBIR/STTR funding over the projects’ lifecycles. Note the two-stage structure: applicants must first clear a short competitive pitch before being invited to submit the full Phase I proposal, so this is not a deadline that can be met from a standing start.

NCI SBIR Innovative Concept Award closes September 21 — and it runs as a contract through SAM.gov

The National Cancer Institute’s SBIR Innovative Concept Phase I award program has a September 21, 2026 deadline. Unlike the NIH omnibus parent announcements, this one is a contract solicitation issued through SAM.gov rather than a grant application through Grants.gov, which means a different submission system, different registration prerequisites, and a deliverables-based statement of work. New Mexico bioscience teams that have only ever submitted NIH grants should budget extra time for the SAM.gov path. Separately, note that HHS now limits SBIR/STTR submissions to nine per small business per fiscal year across HHS and all affiliates.

Cross-agency · policy

Per-firm proposal caps arrive in FY2027 — the shotgun-submission strategy stops working next month

Under the reauthorization, every participating agency must cap the number of proposals a single firm may submit beginning in FY2027, which starts October 1, 2026. Agencies choose their own structure — per fiscal year, per solicitation, or per topic — so a firm working across DoD and DOE may face two different cap regimes, and waivers are limited to 5 percent of topics per year. Agencies are expected to publish FY2027 implementation memos before firms submit. For New Mexico companies that have historically submitted broadly and let hit rate do the work, this is the change that most warrants a strategy conversation with NM FAST this fall.

Cross-agency · policy

Reauthorization context: programs run through FY2031, and unobligated FY2026 money rolls forward

The Small Business Innovation and Economic Security Act (S. 3971) reauthorized SBIR and STTR through September 30, 2031, after the programs lapsed on September 30, 2025. The Senate passed it by voice vote on March 3, 2026 and the House on March 17 by 345–41. Alongside roughly $6 billion in authorized funding, the law creates a Phase II “Strategic Breakthrough” vehicle with a $30 million ceiling over 48 months requiring 100 percent private or non-SBIR matching funds, tightens foreign-risk screening, and lets agencies carry unobligated FY2026 funds into FY2027. That carryover matters concretely: money stranded during the lapse expands the FY2027 award pool rather than disappearing.
