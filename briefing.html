NIH · Seed

NIH Phase IIB “Strategic Breakthrough” award — ceiling up to $30M

PA-27-101, NIH's Phase IIB Strategic Breakthrough parent NOFO (R44, Clinical Trial Optional), implements the new funding vehicle created by April's reauthorization. It is open only to firms that have previously held an NIH SBIR or STTR Phase II award and is designed to bridge the gap between Phase II and commercialization, with total support — direct costs, indirect costs and fee — capped at $30,000,000, though individual institutes set thresholds well below that ceiling. Applicants must secure third-party funding equal to 100% of the NIH funds requested. NIH also reissued PAR-27-098, the SBIR/STTR Commercialization Readiness Pilot (SB1). For New Mexico bioscience companies already holding an NIH Phase II, this is the largest single follow-on lane the program has offered; the matching requirement means investor conversations need to start months ahead of any application.

NASA on a rolling BAA — PY2026 Phase I closed, next appendix ahead

On April 17, 2026 NASA released a Broad Agency Announcement valid through September 30, 2027, replacing the single-window annual solicitation the space-tech community planned around for two decades. Proposals now arrive through phased appendix releases rather than one yearly deadline. The PY2026 Phase I appendices (2026A SBIR, 2026B SBIR, 2026B STTR) opened April 21 and closed May 21, so today's action is monitoring for the next appendix and completing ProSAMS registration in advance — under a rolling BAA the announcement-to-deadline window is short and registration delays are what disqualify firms. Maximum award values rose 50% under the new BAA: Phase I now tops out at $225,000 and Phase II at $1,275,000.

Congress · SBA

Reauthorization in force through FY2031 — and the fiscal-year clock is running

The Small Business Innovation and Economic Security Act (S. 3971) passed the Senate by voice vote March 3 and the House 345–41 on March 17, and was signed into law April 13, 2026, extending SBIR and STTR through September 30, 2031 and ending the six-month lapse that began September 30, 2025. It is the longest extension in the programs' history and authorizes roughly $6B. With FY2026 ending September 30, agencies are compressing solicitations to obligate funds — which is why August and September deadlines are stacking up unusually tightly this year. The act does let agencies carry unobligated FY2026 funds into FY2027, which should soften the usual year-end cliff.

Three changes matter for applicants: the new “Strategic Breakthrough” Phase II vehicle with a $30M ceiling (live at NIH as PA-27-101); expanded foreign-risk and supply-chain due diligence, including screening for affiliations in countries of concern; and, beginning in FY2027, agency-set caps on how many Phase I and Phase II proposals a firm may submit per company, per solicitation, or per topic. That last one is a real strategy shift for New Mexico firms that have relied on submitting broadly — from next fiscal year, every proposal has to count.
