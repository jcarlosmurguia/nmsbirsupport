NIH’s FY2026 parent SBIR and STTR solicitations carry a published standard receipt date of September 5, but because that date falls on a Saturday ahead of Labor Day, applications are accepted through Tuesday, September 8, 2026. DOE’s $10M Genesis Mission Phase I pitch closes September 10 — miss the pitch, lose the cycle

DOE’s FY2026 Phase I Genesis Mission SBIR/STTR opportunity runs on a pitch-first process: a mandatory project pitch is due September 10, 2026 at 2:00pm ET, and only applicants whose pitches are approved are invited to submit a full Phase I application. DOE intends roughly 40 awards totaling about $10 million, representing up to $44 million of cumulative SBIR/STTR funding over the projects’ lifecycles, across four AI-for-science topic areas — scaling the biotechnology revolution, AI for quantum computing and networking, designing materials with predictable functionality, and AI-driven autonomous laboratories. A continuing resolution cleared the House on September 1, pushing the shutdown cliff to December 11

FY2026 ends September 30, and until this week the question was whether agencies would be operating on stopgap funding on October 1. H.R. 6500, as amended by the Senate and passed by the House on September 1, 2026, provides continuing appropriations through December 11, 2026 — funding most programs at FY2026 levels with limited exceptions. For SBIR/STTR applicants the practical read is that solicitation and award machinery should keep running past October 1 rather than stalling, but flat-line funding means agencies are unlikely to expand topic counts or award ceilings mid-cycle, and a second cliff arrives December 11. Note also that agencies have been pushing to issue topics before September 30, which is part of why this month is unusually crowded.

AFWERX & SpaceWERX

AFWERX and SpaceWERX are grouping topics into capability cohorts rather than standalone awards

Both offices reopened solicitations after reauthorization restored program authority, and the structure changed: AFWERX and SpaceWERX now run a cohort-based model that groups related technologies into integrated solutions tied to specific capability gaps, supported by AFRL’s Integrated Product Teams. Named priority areas are collaborative combat aircraft; command, control, communications and battle management; contested logistics; counter-UAS; manufacturing and readiness; small UAS; and weapons technology. Phase I runs to $75,000 (SBIR) or $110,000 (STTR), Phase II to $2 million, Direct-to-Phase II to $1.25 million, and submissions go through the DSIP portal. In FY2025 the programs awarded 1,049 contracts worth $1.4 billion with a 35% transition rate to Air Force customer funding.

National Science Foundation · America’s Seed Fund

NSF’s next full-proposal window is November 4 — but the Project Pitch gate comes first

Under solicitation NSF 26-510, the next Phase I full-proposal window closes November 4, 2026, with a further window on March 4, 2027. NSF is the most sector-agnostic of the major programs and fits all four NM FAST sectors, with Phase I awards up to $305,000 over 6–18 months, Phase II up to $1.25 million, and Fast-Track up to $1,555,000. A New Mexico company aiming at November 4 needs its pitch in now, not in October. A companion instrumentation solicitation, NSF 26-511, runs a parallel deadline structure.

NASA

NASA now runs SBIR/STTR as a rolling BAA, with Phase I ceilings raised to $225K

NASA released a Broad Agency Announcement on April 17, 2026 that is valid through September 30, 2027, replacing the familiar single-window January solicitation, and opened three Phase I appendices — 2026A SBIR, 2026B SBIR and 2026B STTR — with proposals due May 21, 2026. SBA & federal agencies

Per-firm proposal caps arrive with FY2027 — the shotgun-submission strategy stops working next month

Beginning in fiscal year 2027, which starts October 1, agencies set their own proposal caps limiting how many proposals a single business may submit on a per-company, per-solicitation or per-topic basis. Agencies were required to establish those limits no later than 90 days before the start of FY2027, so the numbers should be published or publishing now — worth checking per agency before building a fall submission plan. Waivers exist for time-sensitive topics but may not cover more than 5% of an agency’s topics in a fiscal year. For New Mexico firms the practical effect is that volume stops substituting for fit: fewer, better-targeted proposals is now the only available strategy.

Congress · S. 3971

Reauthorization background: programs run through September 30, 2031 after a six-month lapse

SBIR and STTR authority lapsed and stayed dark for roughly six months before the Small Business Innovation and Economic Security Act (S. 3971) passed the Senate by voice vote on March 3, 2026 and the House 345–41 on March 17, and was signed in April 2026 — reauthorizing both programs through September 30, 2031 and authorizing roughly $6 billion. Alongside the extension it added national security reviews and foreign-risk due diligence that can bar awards to firms with certain foreign ties, and created a new Phase II category, the Strategic Breakthrough Award, worth up to $30 million per project. Every New Mexico applicant should expect ownership and foreign-affiliation questions that did not exist in prior cycles.
