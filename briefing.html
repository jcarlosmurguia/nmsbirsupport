NASA

NASA’s rolling BAA: the next appendix is expected this year, so registration is the whole game

NASA replaced its single annual solicitation window with a Broad Agency Announcement, releasing Phase I subtopics for SBIR, STTR and SBIR Ignite through phased appendices across the year instead of one cycle. The PY2026 Phase I appendices closed May 21, 2026 at 5:00pm ET, and the next appendix is expected later this year. Maximum award values rose 50% under the new structure: Phase I now tops out at $225,000 and Phase II at $1,275,000 — a material change to the competitive math for New Mexico space and sensing companies. NASA also moved submissions into the Proposal Submissions and Award Management System (ProSAMS). With a rolling BAA the announcement-to-deadline window is short, so finishing ProSAMS registration now, rather than when the next appendix drops, is the difference between competing and watching.

Congress · SBA

Thirty-seven days to the end of FY2026 — and the submission caps that arrive with FY2027

The Small Business Innovation and Economic Security Act (S. 3971) cleared the Senate by voice vote March 3 and the House March 17 by 345–41, and was signed into law April 13, 2026 as Public Law 119-83, extending SBIR and STTR through September 30, 2031 — the longest authorization in program history — and ending a six-month lapse that had halted new awards across federal agencies. The law authorizes roughly $6 billion and lets agencies carry unobligated FY2026 funds into FY2027. With FY2026 ending September 30, agencies are compressing solicitations to obligate funds, which is precisely why the deadlines on this page stack into the next five weeks.

Three provisions matter most for planning. A new Phase II “Strategic Breakthrough” vehicle carries a $30 million ceiling. Enhanced foreign-risk screening now applies to every application, covering ownership, patents, employee backgrounds and financial ties to countries of concern. And beginning in FY2027, every participating agency must set a per-firm submission limit — on a fiscal-year, per-solicitation or per-topic basis — published at least 90 days before the fiscal year begins, with topic waivers capped at 5% per year and congressional notice inside 30 days. HHS has already implemented its version at 9 submissions per firm per year. Firms with foreign investors or offshore development should get ahead of the disclosure now rather than at award, and firms that have relied on submission volume need a different strategy starting next October.
