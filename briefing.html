DOE “Genesis Mission” pitch closes in 18 days — and there is no second door

DOE opened its FY26 Phase I SBIR/STTR Genesis Mission opportunity on July 22, 2026 under a mandatory pitch-first process run through its partnership intermediary ConnectWerx: no applicant may go straight to a full Phase I application, and only firms whose pitches are selected are invited to submit. Pitches are due Thursday, September 10, 2026 at 2:00pm ET through the DOE SBIR/STTR Application Hub, and the pitch itself is four questions with defined word limits — short enough to draft this week, decisive enough that a weak one ends the attempt. DOE expects roughly 40 Phase I awards, with awards of up to $250,000 for feasibility work. All four topic areas apply AI to accelerate science: scaling the biotechnology revolution, AI for quantum computing and networking, materials with predictable functionality, and AI-driven autonomous laboratories. That puts advanced energy, advanced computing and bioscience inside a single solicitation and maps unusually well onto national-lab spinout technology in New Mexico.

NSF’s November 4 deadline means the mandatory Project Pitch clock runs out this month

NSF restarted SBIR/STTR under solicitation NSF 26-510 with roughly $250 million across Phase I, Phase II, Fast-Track and Strategic Breakthrough awards. The first post-relaunch full-proposal deadline passed July 27; the next is Wednesday, November 4, 2026, followed by March 4, 2027. Phase I runs up to $305,000 over 6–18 months with no matching-funds requirement — among the most founder-friendly terms in the federal portfolio — and Phase II up to $1.25 million over 24 months. The catch is the gate: an invited Project Pitch is mandatory before any Phase I proposal, pitches reopened June 2, and NSF review takes about three weeks. A firm that has not yet pitched can still make November — but not if September passes first.

NASA

NASA’s rolling BAA: Appendix 2026C is expected later this year, so registration is the whole game

NASA replaced its single annual solicitation window with a Broad Agency Announcement issued April 17, 2026 and valid through September 30, 2027, releasing Phase I subtopics through phased appendices instead. The PY2026 Phase I appendices — 2026A SBIR, 2026B SBIR and 2026B STTR — closed May 21, 2026, and Appendix 2026C is expected later this year. Usefully, NASA limits proposals per appendix and resets the count at the next one, so a firm that submitted to 2026B is not locked out of 2026C. Maximum award values rose 50% under the new structure: Phase I now tops out at $225,000 and Phase II at $1,275,000 — a material change to the competitive math for New Mexico space and sensing companies. With a rolling BAA the announcement-to-deadline window is short, so finishing ProSAMS registration now, rather than when 2026C drops, is the difference between competing and watching.

Congress · SBA

Thirty-eight days to the end of FY2026 — and the submission caps that arrive with FY2027

The Small Business Innovation and Economic Security Act of 2026 (S. 3971) cleared the Senate March 3 by voice vote and the House March 17 by 345–41, and was signed into law April 13, 2026, extending SBIR and STTR through September 30, 2031 — the longest authorization in program history — and ending a six-month lapse that had halted new awards across federal agencies. The law authorizes roughly $6 billion. With FY2026 ending September 30, agencies are compressing solicitations to obligate funds, which is precisely why the deadlines on this page stack into the next five weeks.

Three provisions matter most for planning. A new Phase II “Strategic Breakthrough” funding vehicle carries a $30 million ceiling and applies to agencies whose annual required SBIR expenditures exceed $100 million. Enhanced foreign-risk screening now applies to every application, covering ownership, patents, employee backgrounds and financial ties to countries of concern — agencies must state the basis for any security-based denial, and waivers are capped at 5% of an agency’s topics per year with congressional notification inside 30 days. And beginning in FY2027, each agency sets its own proposal caps limiting how many Phase I and Phase II proposals a company may submit, on a per-company, per-solicitation or per-topic basis; those initial limits were due no later than July 3, 2026. Firms with foreign investors or offshore development should get ahead of the disclosure now rather than at award, and firms that have relied on submission volume need a different strategy starting next October.
